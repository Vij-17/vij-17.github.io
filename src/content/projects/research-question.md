Cultured neurons can adapt their activity when they are embedded in a closed sensory–motor loop, as shown when cortical cultures learned to play a simplified game of Pong. The same neurons in a living brain appear to learn faster, generalise further and remain stable for longer. The question is which ingredient of natural input the culture is missing.

My working hypothesis is that natural sensory input carries temporal and spatial structure (correlated, multiscale and behaviourally contingent) that artificial electrical stimulation does not reproduce. If that is correct, then enriching the statistical structure of stimulation should shift culture learning towards in vivo behaviour.
